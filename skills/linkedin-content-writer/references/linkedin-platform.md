# LinkedIn platform research

Read this reference when the task involves reach, AI detection, distribution advice, hashtags, or link-placement claims. These links are starting points, not permanent rules. Recheck official sources for the current task; the baseline below was verified on October 3, 2026.

## Primary sources

- [Best practices for content created with the help of AI](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496): AI assistance is welcome when content adds value and reflects the member's perspective or expertise. Review assisted text and consider transparency for substantial AI use. Generic, repetitive content without a clear point of view is less likely to be widely distributed.
- [How LinkedIn is Continuing to Tackle AI Slop](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/how-linkedin-is-tackling-ai-slop), September 15, 2026: backend content classifiers limit distribution of low-value material; member feedback informs classifiers. The reported reduction in views of classified material is an aggregate platform result, not a fixed penalty for every AI-assisted post.
- [Keeping conversations real on LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/keeping-conversations-real-on-linkedin), June 4, 2026, originally May 20: describes editorial involvement and an initial generic-content identification result. Generic-content accuracy is not equivalent to accurate proof of AI authorship.
- [Share feedback on AI slop](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a15410007): community feedback informs content quality work; feedback alone does not indicate a policy violation.
- [Engineering the next generation of LinkedIn's Feed](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed), March 12, 2026: describes retrieval and personalized ranking through post information, professional context, and interaction history. Its architecture does not document the separate AI-slop classifier.
- [How LinkedIn Is Improving the Feed](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed), March 12, 2026: identifies repetitive low-substance posts and engagement bait as targets for reduction.
- [Authentic Content and Conversations](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations), March 13, 2026: distinguishes inauthentic engagement, including pods and automated comments, from ordinary participation.

## Interpret the evidence correctly

Report what LinkedIn publicly documents and mark the unknowns. As of this baseline, exact AI-slop model architecture, training data, feature weights, thresholds, and per-post penalties were not disclosed in these sources. Do not infer punctuation rules, detector vendors, or watermark tests from general statements about AI-powered ranking.

Validate the platform and authors of a technical paper before attributing a method to LinkedIn. Papers about Facebook, Instagram, Threads, or general text detection cannot establish LinkedIn's implementation.

Treat creator anecdotes and marketing experiments as weaker evidence than official policy or a well-designed study. Label inference and sampling limitations. If no reliable source establishes an external-link penalty, ideal hashtag count, posting-time advantage, or a detection workaround, leave the claim unresolved rather than inventing a rule.

Use findings to improve substance: audience-relevant examples, clear reasoning, supported details, and genuine discussion. Do not turn platform research into an evasion checklist or claim that stylistic edits prove human authorship.
