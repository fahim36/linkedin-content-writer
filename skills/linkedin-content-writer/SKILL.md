# LinkedIn Content Writer

Write professional content that gives the reader a useful idea before asking for their attention or sending them to a link. Optimize for relevance and substance; reach and AI-detection outcomes cannot be guaranteed.

## Establish the assignment

Identify the topic, audience, purpose, supporting material, and whether the user wants research, a draft, an edit, or publication. Infer reasonable choices from the conversation. Ask only for missing information that changes the substance, then continue independent work.

Use the user's supplied writing and opinions to establish voice. In a software-engineering context, favor actual behavior, trade-offs, failure modes, and usable checks over broad claims about innovation. Treat this as an audience choice, not a restriction to technical posts.

An article link calls for a standalone insight plus a reason to read the article. Read the article or its saved source before composing. Identify one claim, example, or practical takeaway that the linked material actually supports.

## Research when needed

Browse for current topics, statistics, technical uncertainty, and requested research. Prefer the source that owns the claim: original study, official documentation, or company announcement. Verify publication dates and distinguish experimental results, observations, opinions, and recommendations.

For questions about LinkedIn reach, AI content, detection, hashtags, or link placement, read [references/linkedin-platform.md](references/linkedin-platform.md) and verify current official guidance. Keep feed ranking, content-quality classification, and enforcement of automated engagement distinct.

For a substantive research assignment, save a concise cited note containing supported findings, limitations, and unresolved questions in the current workspace's deliverable directory. Stop researching when the post's factual claims are supported and important caveats are understood. Do not turn ordinary edits into a research project.

## Compose the post

Choose a specific point of view the user can reasonably stand behind. Ground first-person claims in information supplied by the user; label constructed examples as illustrative when their origin could be misunderstood. Ask for a real experience only when it would materially improve the post. A lack of anecdotes is a reason to use a factual example, not invent a career story.

Use a clear first line that names the problem, finding, or practical tension. Develop it in connected paragraphs. Add a small list only when the items are parallel and useful. Match length to the request; a focused post usually needs one idea rather than a complete article summary.

Favor familiar words, precise details, and varied sentence lengths. Remove repeated conclusions, inflated claims, vague inspiration, formulaic reversals, and theatrical line breaks. Preserve the user's voice rather than manufacturing typos or quirks to mimic human authorship.

For article promotion, deliver value inside the post, explain what the full article adds, and include its verified public URL. Use a direct link unless the user chooses another placement; claims that hiding links in comments improves reach need evidence.

Include a few relevant hashtags when requested or useful. Select them for topical fit, not an assumed ranking bonus. A genuine question is optional; use it only if a substantive answer would advance the discussion. Avoid agreement bait and artificial engagement campaigns.

When AI substantially supplies the text, follow the platform's current transparency guidance with a brief, accurate disclosure. Treat requests for undetectable writing as requests for specific, natural prose; explain material uncertainty about detection without claiming a bypass.

## Edit before delivery

Check that every factual claim has adequate support, the example's origin is clear, and personal claims are truthful. Verify that the linked article delivers what the post promises. Remove anything that could fit almost any professional topic without alteration.

Read the post as a reader: is there a useful takeaway without opening the link? Does each paragraph advance that takeaway? Is the language something the user could say and defend?

Deliver one polished version unless alternatives were requested or a meaningful choice remains. Keep source notes separate when a citation-heavy post would impair readability. Retain citations in the post when its key claim needs an accessible source.

## Publish only within the user's request

Drafting or invoking this skill alone does not authorize publication. When the user requests posting, use an available purpose-built integration or the supported browser workflow. Honor authorization already given for that post; do not add a second approval step for routine publication. This skill does not grant permission for unrelated posts, cross-posting, comments, messages, ads, subscriptions, or recurring activity.

Inspect the live composer and confirm the author, audience, full text, hashtags, link, and preview. Use a prepared visual only if it improves the post; preserve attribution and accessibility text. Follow applicable tool and platform rules.

Submit once, then verify a success signal and open the resulting post. If submission times out or its outcome is unclear, inspect the feed or recent activity before retrying to avoid duplicates. On a concrete access or upload blocker, preserve the draft and explain the smallest user action needed.

Completion means either a reviewable draft or a verified live post, according to the request. For publication, return the live URL and save a screenshot through the browser tools when available. Report facts about the result, not predicted engagement.
